# Cluj-Napoca city card

The card joins the Transylvania part of the Cities chapter, after Brașov. Cluj-Napoca
joins the trip filter under Transilvania. Three places are appended to the album; no
existing ID or number changes. The introduction reads the city as layers of two
states and several communities rather than repeating the country timeline.

## Evidence consulted

- City history: the English Wikipedia [History of Cluj-Napoca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Cluj-Napoca)
  and [Timeline of Cluj-Napoca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Cluj-Napoca)
  for Roman Napoca, the medieval names and the 1974 addition of "Napoca" under
  Ceaușescu. The precise date of Napoca's municipium status is disputed, so the
  card gives none. The 1918, 1940–1944 and post-war changes follow the general
  country timeline of the guide.
- Piața Unirii: the [St. Michael's Church article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Michael%27s_Church,_Cluj-Napoca)
  for the Roman Catholic Gothic church, the 1862 Gothic Revival tower and the
  monument's position south of the church. The
  [Matthias Corvinus Monument article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Corvinus_Monument)
  and [Mediafax](https://www.mediafax.ro/politic/cluj-udmr-paraseste-sedinta-cl-din-cauza-placutei-cu-textul-lui-iorga-de-la-statuia-lui-matei-corvin-9745388/amp)
  for Fadrusz, 1902, the four generals and the Iorga plaque dispute;
  [Adevărul](https://adevarul.ro/stiri-locale/cluj-napoca/dosarul-in-care-e-cercetat-apostu-ar-viza-placuta-1101116.html)
  for the 2010 removal. Sources disagree on the unveiling day (12 or 14 October)
  and only one dates the plaque to 1932: the card gives the year 1902 and no date
  for the plaque, and does not state whether the plaque is there today.
- Matthias Corvinus House: [Explore Carpathia](https://explorecarpathia.eu/en/romania/kolozsvar-cluj-napoca/birthplace-of-king-matthias-of-hungary)
  and [Actual de Cluj](https://actualdecluj.ro/universitatea-de-arta-deschide-casa-matei-pentru-public-prezinta-selectie-din-fotografiile-donate-de-brancusi/)
  for the address, the birth tradition and the art university's ownership since
  1950 and its gallery. The birth day (January or February 1443) differs between
  sources; the card gives only the year, as a tradition.
- Piața Avram Iancu: [Cluj-Napoca National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluj-Napoca_National_Theatre)
  and [Explore Carpathia](https://explorecarpathia.eu/en/romania/kolozsvar-cluj-napoca/former-hungarian-national-theatre-romanian-national-theatre)
  for Fellner & Helmer, the 1906 opening as the Hungarian national theatre and its
  1919 change; [Actual de Cluj](https://actualdecluj.ro/?p=46959) for the
  interwar architecture competitions, the 1923–1933 cathedral and the park that
  preceded the square.

No opening hours, ticket prices or interior access are introduced.

# Bicaz Gorges and Ceahlău landscape card

Eighth and last card of the Natura chapter, shown for Moldavia and Transilvania:
the gorge is the road between Neamț (Moldavia) and Harghita (Transilvania). Three
places are appended to the album: the Bicaz gorges at the Gâtul Iadului and the
Toaca peak (Moldavia), and Lacu Roșu (Harghita, Transilvania). With this card
the chapter is shown for all four regions (`on: all`).

## Evidence consulted

- Gorges: [Bicaz Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicaz_Canyon),
  [Dangerous Roads](https://dangerousroads.org/eastern-europe/romania/4946-bicaz-gorge.html)
  and [Birding Places](https://www.birdingplaces.eu/birdingplaces/romania/bicaz-gorge)
  for the DN12C through about 8 km of gorge (one source says about 10 km, from
  Lacu Roșu to Bicaz-Chei), limestone walls about 300 m high, the Gâtul Iadului
  with the road under overhanging rock, the stalls of Romanian and Hungarian
  craftsmen, the Cheile Bicazului-Hășmaș National Park in Neamț and Harghita.
  Wikipedia's "passageway between Moldavia and Transylvania" is uncited; the
  card says the road joins the two counties, which a map shows.
- Lacu Roșu: [Red Lake](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Lake_(Romania)>),
  [Romanian Wikipedia](https://www.wikiwand.com/ro/Lacul_Roșu),
  [BZI](https://www.bzi.ro/de-ce-merita-vizitat-lacul-rosu-iata-care-este-legenda-unuia-dintre-cele-mai-indragite-lacuri-din-romania-5446316)
  and [Orange Centenar](https://www.orange.ro/info/centenar/article/lacul-rosu/2768707)
  for the landslide dam (1837, or 1838 with the earthquake of 23 January 1838:
  the card gives both), the drowned fir forest, the trunks preserved by water
  rich in calcium carbonate and iron oxide, 983 m, the Hungarian name and its
  legend.
- Ceahlău: [Ceahlău Massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceahl%C4%83u_Massif)
  for Toaca (1,904 m) and Ocolașul Mare (1,907 m), Bistrița and Lake Bicaz to the
  east, access from Durău, the park's fees and camping rules, the Dochia chalet
  and the weather station at Toaca; [Agerpres](https://agerpres.ro/2018/10/15/reportaj-masivul-ceahlau---un-munte-de-legende-si-credinta-vizitat-anual-de-zeci-de-mii-de-turisti--193377),
  [TVR Info](https://tvrinfo.ro/6-august-schimbarea-la-fata-a-domnului-si-probejania/)
  and [BZI](https://www.bzi.ro/evenimente-care-vor-avea-loc-in-arhiepiscopia-iasilor-in-perioada-6-12-august-2023-4774223)
  for the monastery church of 1992–1993 near Dochia and Toaca, dedicated to the
  Transfiguration, and the pilgrims of 6 August; [Newsweek România](https://newsweek.ro/timp-liber/enigma-piramidei-holografice-straniul-fenomen-din-ceahlau-se-crede-ca-e-muntele-sacru-al-dacilor)
  and [EVZ](https://evz.ro/fenomenul-paranormal-care-atrage-pe-data-de-6-august-mii-de-turisti-pe-muntele-ceahlau.html)
  for the "holographic pyramid" seen from Toaca at sunrise: the card explains it
  as the mountain's shadow and does not take up "sacred mountain of the Dacians"
  (B2, B7). [Visit Neamț](https://www.visitneamt.com/tag/descriptio-moldaviae-dimitrie-cantemir)
  for Cantemir's _Descriptio Moldaviae_: the mountain on his map, its hermits
  and the comparison with Olympus.

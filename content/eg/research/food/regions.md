# Gastronomia: the regions

One card per region with a specialty, each with one trace `where: 'region'`. The same C1 check
as in `common.md` (2026-11-24: pages opened and read in full).

## Basso Egitto — Sayadeya (common)

Wikipedia, Sunheron ("classic Alexandrian fish-and-rice dish"), Chez Nermine (Nermine Mansour).

- Fish (grey mullet or sea bream) with rice browned by caramelised onions: Sunheron, Wikipedia;
  the rice with cumin and cinnamon: Chez Nermine. Alexandria: Sunheron, Wikipedia.
- The name from sayyad, "fisherman": Wikipedia only; kept as a translation of the menu word.
- **Corrected**: Port Said and Suez removed (Wikipedia only / no page).
- Dishes of the same name on the Levant coast: [Asif](https://asif.org/en/recipes/sayadieh-jaffa-style-fish-over-rice/)
  (Nadir Abu-Seif: Jaffa, "from Tripoli in Libya to Saida in Lebanon"), Blanche Shaheen on
  [Arab America](https://arabamerica.com/sayadieh-the-classic-fish-and-rice-dish-of-the-levant)
  (2024: Lebanon, Akka, Gaza), Wikipedia. Resolved 2026-11-24; the text says "dal Libano a Gaza"
  (B6: no places outside Egypt named one by one).

## Alto Egitto — Eish shamsi (now rare)

Nawaya on Google Arts & Culture (story and photo caption: one source), Slow Food Ark of Taste,
Egyptian Streets (Marina Makary, 2022), Wikipedia as check.

- Sourdough with a starter from the last baking: Nawaya, Slow Food ("sourdough"). Rising in the
  sun: Nawaya, Egyptian Streets, Wikipedia. Mud/clay ovens: Nawaya, Slow Food. Tomb paintings:
  Slow Food, Wikipedia. Upper Egypt only: Nawaya, Egyptian Streets. At risk: Slow Food (Ark of
  Taste), Wikipedia. Rare in city bakeries, made at home: Egyptian Streets, Wikipedia.
- Left out: the clay discs with bran (Nawaya only).
- **Rarity changed to rare**: the sources describe a bread made at home in villages, not one in
  every restaurant (B12: "rara quando bisogna cercarla"). The "Come trovarla" no longer names
  Sohag or restaurants in Luxor and Aswan (no page).

## Alto Egitto — Karkadè (moved from Nubia)

Egyptian Streets (Mona Abdou, 2022), Saudi Aramco World (John Feeney, 2001), Pilgrimaps
(Silvia Costantini, 2026).

- Calyces, sweetened, hot in winter and iced in summer: all three. Grown around Aswan and Qena
  and in the Fayyum: Aramco World, Pilgrimaps (fields between Kom Ombo and Daraw). Ramadan iftar:
  Egyptian Streets, Aramco World.
- **Region changed** from Nubia to Alto Egitto: the trace was in Nubia as "the Nubian villages'
  welcome drink", which no page says; both sources put the crop around Aswan and Qena, in Alto
  Egitto. Nubia has no food card now (B13 sets no food quota per region).
- **The trace is the calyces** (2026-11-25): the photo is the dried calyces sold by weight at the
  market, not a glass, so it is not a third glass of tea after the shai. "Calyces sold by weight
  in markets": Pilgrimaps (Cairo souks) only, as before; it stays in the «Come trovarla» as the
  place to look, not as a fact of the text.
- **Removed** 2026-11-24: offered to guests on arrival. Pilgrimaps only; the second candidates
  (a ckbk cookbook, Egypt Today) answer 403, Egyptian Streets does not say it, and tour listings
  (Airbnb) are not sources. "Calyces sold in markets": Pilgrimaps (Cairo souks) — in the "Come
  trovarla" only.

## Deserto occidentale — Datteri di Siwa (common)

Slow Food Presidium, FAO GIAHS, Daily News Egypt (14 October 2026).

- Siwi the main variety: Slow Food, Daily News Egypt. Hand harvest climbing the palms: Slow Food,
  FAO. From early autumn: Slow Food (Siwi and Azzawi mature early October). Fresh or oven-dried:
  Slow Food; paste: Daily News Egypt (the Siwa factory). GIAHS since 2016: FAO.
- **Corrected**: agwa (no page: Atlas Obscura 403), the Frehi and Azzawi varieties and the
  palm-fibre belt (Slow Food only), the October festival (an event with a changing date, B13)
  removed.

## Sinai — no card

The Bedouin tea is out of the album (2026-11-25: a duplicate of the shai, see

  (Atmos) → neither in the text. Egypt Independent on the St Katherine medicinal plants could not
  be opened (403).

## Mar Rosso — Jabana (rare)

Nawaya on Google Arts & Culture (story and photo caption: one source), Mongabay (Victoria
Schneider, 27 October 2025). Egypt Independent and Egypt Today could not be opened (403).

Second source found 2026-11-24: [MarsaAlam.com, "Meet The Ababda"](https://marsaalam.com/?p=24838)
(no author or date; a "2026 Update" note).

- Coffee of the Ababda: Nawaya, Mongabay, MarsaAlam.com. Black coffee with ginger: Nawaya,
  Mongabay, MarsaAlam.com (beans and dried ginger ground together).
- Green beans roasted fresh (MarsaAlam.com: "over hot coals"; Nawaya: "on fire"): both. The pot
  called gabbana, once clay: Nawaya, MarsaAlam.com ("soapstone or clay"). Tiny cups, several
  rounds, usually at least three: Nawaya, MarsaAlam.com.
- **Corrected**: the Bishareen (Nawaya only in the text read), "odd numbers" (Nawaya only), the
  fibre filter (no page) and "Beja peoples" removed.
- **Where to find it**: al-Qula'an, the Ababda village in Wadi el-Gemal that welcomes visitors
  with ginger coffee (Mongabay, 2025; MarsaAlam.com names its Ababda-run café); Sharm el-Luli,
  where the Ababda "sometimes" run an eatery serving gabbana (MarsaAlam.com). Two pages, for two
  places in the same park: enough for the "Come trovarla", to check again before publishing
  (B9: places that change).

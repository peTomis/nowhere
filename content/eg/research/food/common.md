## Koshari

Egyptian Streets (Mona Bassel, 2021) and Gulf News (Sharon Benjamin, 2021), independent wording.

- Rice, lentils, pasta, chickpeas, fried onions, vinegar-garlic sauce: both. Tomato sauce:
  Egyptian Streets' recipe; Gulf News names shatta and da'ah.
- Meatless: both ("vegetarian").
- Khichri brought by Indian troops with the British: both, **dates differ** (Egyptian Streets
  "early 1900s following the first world war"; Gulf News "the 1800s") → the text gives no date.
- Pasta from the Italians in Egypt: both (Gulf News: Suez Canal workers; Egyptian Streets: "some
  accounts").
- **Corrected**: the first draft said "after 1882" (no page says it), "eaten on Coptic fast
  days" (no page says it) and gave the mujaddara theory (Gulf News only). All three removed.

## Molokhia

Egyptian Streets (2020, quoting Paul Walker, _Caliph of Cairo_, 2009), Wikipedia _Mulukhiyah_.

- Corchorus olitorius, viscous, chicken or rabbit, rice or bread: both.
- Al-Maqrizi records al-Hakim's ban: both. Mu'awiya: Egyptian Streets (Walker's reason),
  Wikipedia (his favourite dish, per al-Maqrizi). **Date differs**: 1004 (Egyptian Streets) vs
  7 Muharram 395 AH = 1005 (Wikipedia) → the Context says "Inizio XI sec.".
- **Corrected**: the punishment of sellers (1009, 1014) is Egyptian Streets only → removed.
- The ta'leya (garlic and coriander fried in ghee or butter, added at the end): [SBS Food](https://www.sbs.com.au/food/recipe/molokheya-egyptian-style/mvbb1pymx)
  (Sam Habib, 2015), [Chez Nermine](https://cheznermine.com/2023/09/06/molokhia-egyptian-jute-mellow-soup-شوربة-الملوخية/)
  ("Tasha, aka Taklia"). Resolved 2026-11-24. Walker's book itself not read.

  ministry's "Historical Cairo" (al-Qata'i, the third capital).

- Over twenty million in the metropolitan area: Wikipedia (22 million), Rough Guides (nearly 20).

## Traces

- **Ibn Tulun**: 876–879, Abbasid governor, Samarra model, brick and stucco, oldest mosque in its
  original form, spiral minaret, the paper legend: Wikipedia, Rough Guides. Climbing the minaret:
  travel pages in the search (on site, sometimes a fee). Gayer-Anderson entrance: Rough Guides.
- **Bab Zuwayla**: 1092, Badr al-Gamali; al-Mu'ayyad's minarets on the towers (1415–1422):
  ministry, Wikipedia. Tuman Bay hanged in 1517: Wikipedia, cairo.gov.eg (search). The climb with
  a ticket: travel pages (Audiala, Frommer's) in the search.
- **Khayamiya**: covered market of about 1650, Radwan Bey, the only historic covered market street,
  the last market of khayamiya textiles: Wikipedia, Archnet, TRC Leiden.
- **Tanoura**: a Sufi-derived dance by a state troupe at the al-Ghuri buildings (1504–1505):
  Wikipedia (Wikala), Pure Nile Tours and tour listings. Days and times disagree between sources
  (Saturday, Monday, Wednesday; 19:00, 19:30 or 20:00): the text gives none. **Changes over time**:
  recheck before publishing.
- **Qaitbay**: 1470–1474 in the Northern Cemetery; the carved star-and-arabesque dome: Wikipedia,
  Archnet, MIT. People living in the cemetery: Wikipedia (City of the Dead). Photo rule: country
  sheet §13 (FCDO).
- **Cave Church**: dug in the 1970s by the Zabbaleen, Coptic garbage collectors of Mokattam;
  Simon the Tanner and the moving of the mountain as Coptic tradition: Slate/Atlas Obscura,
  Wikipedia (Zabbaleen), search results (Egypt Today, CairoScene). "Thousands": CairoScene (2,000)
  and Slate; the 20,000 seats of some pages left out.
- **Empain**: 1907–1911, Alexandre Marcel, Indian style, reinforced concrete, abandoned, bought
  by the state (2005) and restored: ministry, Wikipedia. Open daily 9–18: ministry page.

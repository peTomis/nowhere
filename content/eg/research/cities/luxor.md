# Città: Luxor (Alto Egitto)

City card `eg.luxor`, six traces. Pages read on 2026-11-25: Rough Guides "Luxor"; the ministry's
Luxor Temple page; the Museo Egizio's page on the Turin Strike Papyrus; World Monuments Fund
"New Gourna Village"; Elizabeth Hawksley on the Winter Palace; Wikipedia as check (Luxor, Great
Hypostyle Hall, Abu Haggag Mosque, Colossi of Memnon, Deir el-Medina, Hassan Fathy, Winter Palace
Hotel).

- East bank of the living, west bank of the dead; New Kingdom capital: Rough Guides, Wikipedia.
- **Karnak**: grown over many centuries; Seti I's hypostyle hall: Rough Guides. 134 columns, the
  12 central ones higher, the clerestory: Wikipedia only: **to recheck**.
- **Luxor Temple**: Amenhotep III and Ramesses II, the sphinx avenue to Karnak, the obelisk in
  the Place de la Concorde since 1836: ministry. The mosque of Abu el-Haggag inside the court of
  Ramesses II, worship for over three thousand years: Wikipedia and the Commons descriptions
  (Marc Ryckaert). The "door hanging in the air" of the plan: no page, left out.
- **Colossi**: Amenhotep III's temple, the earthquake of 27 BC, the dawn sound and Eos, Septimius
  Severus's restoration about AD 199: Rough Guides; Roman graffiti: Wikipedia.
- **Deir el-Medina**: the village of the tomb builders; the strike under Ramesses III, the papyrus
  in Turin: Museo Egizio, Wikipedia, Guinness (search: "first recorded strike").
- **New Gourna**: Hassan Fathy, mud brick and Nubian vaults, the villagers' refusal, "Architecture
  for the Poor" calling it a failure: Wikipedia (Hassan Fathy); degraded state: World Monuments
  Fund, the UNESCO safeguarding page and a 2024 paper (search). No image: Fathy died in 1989.
- **Winter Palace**: built for winter travellers with Thomas Cook: Wikipedia (inaugurated 1907)
  and an EFE report (1886) disagree on the year: the text says "inizio del Novecento". Carter's
  announcement at the hotel: Hawksley and other pages say so "by tradition"; the text says so.
  Access for non-guests: no page.

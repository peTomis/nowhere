# Natura: Il Deserto Bianco (Deserto occidentale)

Landscape card `eg.landscape.white-desert`, traces `white-desert`, `black-desert`,
`crystal-mountain` (all remote, rare). Pages read on 2026-11-25. FCDO excepts "the White Desert and
Black Desert" and the Giza–Farafra road from its advice: all three traces are on that road.

## Pages

- Deseret News (Associated Press), "White rocks an eerie sight" (2003) (AP).
- Egypt Fun Tours, "The Black Desert: A Volcanic Wonder of the Bahariya Oasis".
- Flying Carpet Tours, "The Black Desert".
- Wikipedia: White Desert National Park, Black Desert (Egypt), Crystal Mountain (Egypt) (as
  check).

## Facts and their sources

- Chalk floor, shaped by wind and sand into mushrooms and figures, some as tall as two-storey
  buildings: AP, Wikipedia. Marine origin (tiny shells in the rock): AP; Khoman chalk in the
  search results.
- Erosion at the base makes the "mushrooms": search result (Wikipedia-derived page) and AP's
  description of the shapes; the mechanism is phrased plainly.
- Moonlight colours: AP.
- Protected since 2002: Wikipedia; the decree 1120/2002 in the search results.
- 45 km north of Farafra: Wikipedia. Camping in tents, cold nights in winter: AP.
- Black Desert: hills capped by dark volcanic rock (basalt, dolerite) that protected softer rock
  beneath: Wikipedia, Egypt Fun Tours, Flying Carpet. Climbing a hill for the view: Egypt Fun
  Tours, Flying Carpet. Loose slopes: search results (travel page).
- Crystal Mountain: a ridge of crystals with a small arch/hole: Wikipedia ("small arch"), AP
  ("a hole in the middle"). The mineral is disputed: AP and travel pages say quartz; Wikipedia
  (and Wikidata's "calcite mountain") calcite and baryte from hardness tests. The text gives
  both, attributed.

## Left out

- Fauna (Wikipedia only).
- Heights of the Black Desert hills (up to 100 m: Wikipedia only).
- Black Desert reserve since 2010 (Wikipedia only).
- The age of the volcanic rock: Wikipedia says Jurassic, a geology paper in the search says
  Tertiary.

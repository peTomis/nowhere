# Natura: Le acque di Siwa (Deserto occidentale)

Landscape card `eg.landscape.siwa-waters` (planned as «Siwa e il Mare di Sabbia»), traces
`salt-lakes` (nearby, common) and `cleopatras-spring` (nearby, common). Pages read on 2026-11-25.

## The card changed: the Great Sand Sea is out

The plan's trace 109, «Le dune del Grande Mare di Sabbia», fails the safety check of the country
sheet (§13). The UK FCDO page (read 2026-11-25) advises against all but essential travel to the
Western Desert, with exceptions that name "the oasis town of Siwa" and the Marsa Matruh–Siwa road,
not the desert around it, and it advises against the Bahariya–Siwa road. A 4x4 trip into the dunes
south of Siwa (Bir Wahed and beyond) is outside the exception. The trace is in
`../album/excluded-traces.md`; the Sand Sea stays as one line of the introduction.

Cleopatra's spring (planned 107, under the Cleopatra card) becomes the card's second trace: a
spring of the oasis, so the card is about water. The Cleopatra card, when written, lists the same
trace (D6: the same trace, not a copy).

Salt pools and the spring are 3–10 km from the town centre, inside the oasis: read as within "the
oasis town of Siwa", as the album's other Siwa traces (Shali, the oracle temple). **To recheck**
against the Farnesina page, still not read (it renders with JavaScript).

## Facts and their sources

- About 50 km from the Libyan border: NASA, Wikipedia.
- Down to about 20 m below sea level: NASA (20 m), Wikipedia (−19 m).
- Almost no rain (about 10 mm a year): NASA; water from springs (NASA; Wikipedia: about 200
  springs, "centinaia di sorgenti"), fed by groundwater (NASA; the aquifer's name, Nubian
  Sandstone, and "one of the largest" are NASA only: not in the text).
- Dates and olives: NASA, Wikipedia.
- Irrigation runoff pools in the salt lakes; salt builds up in the soil and yields fall: NASA
  (yields −46% olives, −55% dates 2000–2011: not used), Wikipedia (salt lakes fed by agricultural
  drainage).
- Salt harvested in evaporation ponds: NASA; TBS and the search results (industrial production).
- Floating in the salt pools: NASA ("bobbing in the dense waters"), TBS. That the pools were dug by
  the extraction is in travel pages only: left out.
- Great Sand Sea south of the oasis, reaching Libya: Wikipedia (both pages).
- Cleopatra's spring: a natural spring, ancient (Wikipedia); the Cleopatra tradition as a popular
  belief (the Commons descriptions of the photos and Wikipedia's "Cleopatra's Bath"); "fonte del
  Sole", Ain el-Shams: Commons descriptions (Sara Nabih, Roland Unger). Cafés around it:
  OpenStreetMap ("Cleopatra spring and coffee shops") and the Commons photos.

## Left out

- The 95% salinity of the pools (travel blogs only).
- Swimming dress: the "vestiti" advice is general (Siwa is conservative: Wikipedia, culture).

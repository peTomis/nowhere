# Natura: Il golfo di Aqaba (Sinai)

Landscape card `eg.landscape.gulf-of-aqaba`, traces `ras-mohammed` (nearby, common), `blue-hole`
(nearby, rare), `nabq-mangroves` (nearby, rare). Pages read on 2026-11-25. All three are on the
coast, outside FCDO's advice (coasts excepted).

## Facts and their sources

- Ras Mohammed between the gulfs of Suez and Aqaba: KBA, Wikipedia.
- The Gulf of Aqaba on the fault between the African and Arabian plates (Dead Sea Transform), deep
  and narrow: Wikipedia (Dead Sea Transform), the Leeds and geology pages in the search ("widest
  and deepest in the gulf"); Wikipedia (Blue Hole): the floor drops past 1,000 m off Dahab. No
  depth figure in the text.
- Fringing reefs reached from the shore: KBA ("coral reefs fringe the headland"), Wikipedia
  (fringing reefs; Blue Hole "accessible from shore").
- Diving fame during the Israeli occupation: Wikipedia (Blue Hole: first dives 1968, reputation
  under the occupation); Sacred Land (occupation 1967–1982, for the dates).
- Ras Mohammed 1983, Egypt's first/oldest protected area: KBA (decree 1068/1983, "Egypt's oldest
  protected area"), Wikipedia, and the Geotema and EEAA documents in the search. Nabq 1992: KBA,
  Wikipedia.
- Bedouin fisheries: Wikipedia (Nabq), tourism today: KBA (tourism development).
- Ras Mohammed: uplifted coral cliffs, sandy bays (KBA); mangrove stand (KBA, Wikipedia); cracks
  from earthquakes, with water (Wikipedia, and the Commons photo _The Crack Ras Mohammed_: **one
  page**); the name from a bearded profile (Wikipedia: "it is said": in the conditional).
- Blue Hole: over 100 m deep, the Arch at 55 m (Wikipedia, IFLScience and Slate in the search);
  the deadliest dive site by reputation (Wikipedia "reputed", Slate/Atlas Obscura); memorial
  plaques (Slate, Cairo Scene, in the search); calm water, snorkelling deaths at the surface
  (Wikipedia).
- Nabq: mangroves at the mouth of Wadi Kid, about 7 km of shore (KBA), "Shorat el-Manqata" (KBA);
  only Avicennia marina in South Sinai (EJABF); among the most northerly in the world (KBA,
  BirdLife in the search); breeding waterbirds (KBA: spoonbill, herons, osprey; the text keeps it
  general).

# Romania: trace texts made coherent

On 2026-11-14 the texts of the guide's 126 traces (`traces` in `content/ro/it/names.ts`) were
reviewed for consistency and concreteness. Most texts already met the bar and were left as they
were; thirteen were rewritten, and small typographic and `place` fixes were made across the
album.

## Style applied

- `text`: 2–4 sentences, present tense, plain voice; lead with what makes the place matter, end
  with what to look at. Abstract closing lines ("racconta…", "non una testimonianza di
  isolamento") replaced by one concrete detail.
- `place`: always "Town, Region" (or "Area, Region"); "distretto di …" dropped from `place`
  (the county stays in "Come trovarla"), Bucovina used as region for Putna and Voroneț, the
  Mehedinți traces under Valacchia as in `structure.ts`. Template literals (`places[…]`) wherever
  the name exists, plain strings otherwise.
- `distance`: always "a circa N km da …", with `places[…]`.
- Measures with digits use "m" (not "metri"); "chilometri" spelled out in prose.
- Food traces keep no `text`: their cards already describe the dish.

## Rewritten texts and their sources

- **Stephen the Great's tomb, equestrian statue (Suceava)**: tightened, no new facts.
- **Timișoara, Piața Unirii**: tightened; "after 1716" comes from the Timișoara card.
- **Stavropoleos**: founder Ioannikios (Ioanichie) Stratonikeas, a Greek monk, built the church in
  1724 inside his inn, whose income kept the monastery; inn and annexes demolished at the end of
  the 19th century; the early-20th-century building by Ion Mincu; fragments of wall paintings from
  churches demolished under communism kept there.
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavropoleos_Monastery)
- **Romanian Athenaeum**: the public fundraising "Dați un leu pentru Ateneu!" (a 28-year effort);
  Costin Petrescu's 75 × 3 m fresco from Trajan's conquest to Greater Romania (1918).
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Athenaeum)
- **Brașov Council House**: the 23 December 1420 agreement with the furriers' guild for a council
  chamber above its sales vault; the 58 m "trumpeters' tower", guards blowing the hours and
  alarms; court and prison in the basement; museum since 1950.
  [Wikipedia (ro)](https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_Sfatului_din_Bra%C8%99ov)
- **Black Church**: the name "Black Church" only from the end of the 19th century; the fire of
  1689 as its traditional explanation, recent studies (Judit Petki) pointing to pollution of the
  industrial city instead; carpets donated by Saxon merchants between the 15th and 17th
  centuries, the largest collection of "Transylvanian rugs"; Buchholz organ of 1839 with 4,000
  pipes. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biserica_Neagr%C4%83)
  The old text said the 1689 fire "badly damaged roof and interiors": the same article says this
  theory "has been proven wrong" while other sources repeat it, so the new text gives the fire
  only as the tradition behind the name.
- **Brukenthal Palace**: collections opened in 1817; Brukenthal as governor.
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brukenthal_National_Museum). The small portrait
  attributed to Jan van Eyck, c. 1430, 22.5 × 16.6 cm, held by the Brukenthal museum
  ("Man in a Blue Cap"). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrait_of_a_Man_with_a_Blue_Chaperon)
- **Bridge of Lies**: built 1859–1860, the first cast-iron bridge in today's Romania; the name
  from _Liegebrücke_ ("lying-down bridge") heard as _Lügenbrücke_.
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_of_Lies)
- **Sighișoara Clock Tower**: about 64 m; town hall until 1656; Baroque roof rebuilt in 1677 after
  the fire of 1676; the figures (Pax, Justice, drummer on the upper-town side; seven day figures
  on a platform turning at midnight on the lower-town side); museum inside.
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sighi%C8%99oara_Clock_Tower)
- **Covered stairway**: 17th century, originally 300 steps, now 175.
  [Visit Mureș](https://visitmures.com/en/sighisoara/built-heritage/castles-citadels-architectural-curiosities/scholars-stairs-sighisoara).
  Sources disagree on the year (1642, or 1660–1662) and on when it was shortened (1842 or 1849):
  the text gives neither.
- **Iași Palace of Culture**: built inside the perimeter of the medieval princely court, on the
  foundations of the old princely palace; 298 large rooms; the tower's carillon of 8 bells plays
  "Hora Unirii" every hour.
  [Wikipedia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Culture_(Ia%C8%99i)>)
  The reinforced-concrete structure was already in the text; not confirmed by this page.
- **Three Hierarchs**: built 1637–1639 by Vasile Lupu as a princely necropolis, gilded stone
  decoration, relics of St Paraskeva arriving from Constantinople in June 1641, the 1642 synod in
  the refectory (today the Gothic Hall), Dimitrie Cantemir and Alexandru Ioan Cuza buried there.
  [Wikipedia (ro)](https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%83n%C4%83stirea_Sfin%C8%9Bii_Trei_Ierarhi_din_Ia%C8%99i)
  **Correction**: the old text said Metropolitan Varlaam was buried here in 1641. Varlaam
  consecrated the church (1639) and received the relics (1641); he died in 1657 at Secu
  Monastery, where his remains are.
  [Wikipedia (ro)](https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varlaam_Mo%C8%9Boc)

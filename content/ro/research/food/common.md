# Gastronomia: the whole country

First part of the Gastronomia chapter (chapter 6): five food cards shown for
every trip, each with one trace `where: 'anywhere'` (on the album's first page,
after the cover, the flag and the coat of arms). Their regional variants will be
added to these cards as traces when the regional parts are written (ciorbă de
burtă, ciorbă rădăuțeană, borș lipovenesc; pălincă, horincă; balmoș).

## Evidence consulted

- Mămăligă: [Mămăligă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%83m%C4%83lig%C4%83)
  for the maize porridge cooked in a ceaun, millet before maize, maize in the
  principalities from the early to mid 17th century (Iorga; attributions to
  princes vary and are left out), its role in the famines of the 17th and 18th
  centuries, cheese and smântână, fish with mujdei, bulz and the thread used to
  cut it. To check in a second source: the dating (Banat 1692, principalities
  17th century).
- Sarmale: [Sarma](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarma_(food)>) for the Turkish
  name ("wrap"), the Ottoman cuisine, pork, rice and onion, sour or fresh cabbage
  and vine leaves, Christmas and Easter, mămăligă and smântână. Weddings and "the
  national dish" are left out.
- Ciorbă and borș: [Romanian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_cuisine),
  [Chorba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorba) and
  [Playtech](https://playtech.ro/2026/ce-inseamna-ciorba-in-turca-si-cum-i-s-a-schimbat-sensul-in-romana/)
  for the Turkish çorba, borș as fermented wheat bran, the other souring agents,
  borș for the soup itself in Moldavia, lovage and smântână.
- Cozonac: [Cozonac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cozonac) and
  [Baking Heritage](https://bakingheritage.com/history/cozonac-the-festive-sweet-bread-that-rose-through-empires-and-time)
  for the yeast bread with walnuts, cocoa and lokum, Easter and Christmas, and
  similar breads in neighbouring countries.
- Țuică: [Țuică](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C8%9Auic%C4%83) for plums only,
  6–8 weeks of fermentation, the copper still, 24–40% once distilled, stronger
  twice distilled with regional names (pălincă, horincă), the shot before the
  meal and the glass offered to guests. "National drink" and the production
  figures are left out.

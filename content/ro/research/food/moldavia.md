# Gastronomia: Moldavia

Fourth part of the Gastronomia chapter, shown for Moldavia: four food cards,
four traces with `where: 'region'`. Ciorbă rădăuțeană is also a variant on the
whole-country Ciorbă e borș card (same trace, D6).

## Evidence consulted

- Tochitură: [Tochitură](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochitur%C4%83),
  [Newsweek România](https://newsweek.ro/timp-liber/tochitura-moldoveneasca-cum-se-face-corect-si-ce-greseli-sa-eviti-p)
  and [Table Journey](https://tablejourney.com/dish/tochitura-moldoveneasca/)
  for pork and smoked sausage slow-cooked in their fat, the name from _a topi_,
  mămăligă, fried egg and salty sheep cheese (telemea or brânză de burduf).
- Poale-n brâu: [Adevărul](https://adevarul.ro/stiri-locale/galati/cum-se-prepara-celebrele-placinte-poale-n-brau-1797557.html),
  [România TV](https://www.romaniatv.net/reteta-de-poale-n-brau-ca-la-bunica-secretul-placintelor-moldovenesti-preferatele-lui-ion-creanga_8058466.html)
  and [BZI](https://www.bzi.ro/branzoaice-cu-branza-dulce-poale-n-brau-ca-la-bunica-acasa-4312964)
  for the yeast dough folded from the edges, sweet cheese with raisins, the
  salted version with dill, the name from the folding, and Creangă.
- Ciorbă rădăuțeană: [G4Media](https://www.g4media.ro/ciorba-radauteana-implineste-40-de-ani-de-cand-a-intrat-in-meniurile-restaurantelor-a-fost-inventata-de-o-bucatareasa-din-bucovina.html),
  [Newsweek România](https://newsweek.ro/timp-liber/ciorba-radauteana-in-topul-celor-mai-bune-supe-din-lume-a-fost-inventata-la-insistentele-unui-sot)
  and [Puterea](https://www.puterea.ro/?p=278970) for Cornelia Dumitrescu at
  Rădăuți, chicken, yolks, smântână, garlic, vinegar or lemon, a lighter cousin
  of tripe soup. Dates go from the 1970s to 1982 (recipe registered 14 February
  1982 according to Romanian Wikipedia): the card gives the range. The world
  rankings are left out (B7).
- Cotnari: [Bursa](https://www.bursa.ro/cotnari-campionul-vinurilor-romanesti-60361538),
  [Știrile ProTV](https://stirileprotv.ro/divers/wine-trips-romania-cotnari-pe-urmele-soiurilor-romanesti.html)
  and [AIS Vitae](https://vitae.aisitalia.it/?p=12016) for the Romanian
  varieties only, grasă and frâncușă grown only at Cotnari, frâncușă dry and the
  others sweet, the DOC and Cantemir praising Grasă de Cotnari. "Stephen the
  Great's vineyard" is left out: no source opened.
